Baseline — the P1 Checklist (0.1–0.54)

The original ruleset, verbatim in substance from niv-to-phase1's phase1-rules.md. Unhighlighted — this is the fixed starting point everything below builds on.

0.1No third-person pronouns (except "each-other"); 1st/2nd-person pronouns get a parenthetical referent — "I(John)".
0.2Level 2/3 ontology words only via pairing, explication, or a "(complex)" alternate — never bare.
0.3Hyphenated ontology words stay hyphenated; default tense is "discourse", override with underscore notation.
0.3bOne verb per clause; subordinate clauses in square brackets; left and right bracket counts must match.
0.4No more than four levels of embedded brackets — simplify or split the sentence instead.
0.5Place subordinate clauses/adverbial PPs so the sentence has the intended scope.
0.6A prepositional phrase that modifies a noun goes in a relative clause on that noun, not left dangling.
0.7Start every relative clause with "that"/"who"/"whom" — never with "that" on an object-complement clause.
0.8Never use "when"/"where" as a relativizer — "the place [that... in]", not "the place [where...]".
0.9Avoid repeating a noun across main and object-complement clause; put it once, in the patient clause.
0.10Use "that" rather than "the" when re-referring to a previously mentioned noun.
0.11Mark implicit info with parentheses (clauses) or underscore (phrase before the underscore) — don't hide a head if its modifiers aren't also implicit.
0.12Precede quotations with speaker + verb ("say"/"ask"); mark both implicit with "_implicitNecessary" on each.
0.13Passives include the agent with "by" — mark it "_implicitActiveAgent" if the agent itself is implicit.
0.14Rhetorical questions get "(rhetorical)"/"(yesrhetorical)"/"(norhetorical)" plus a "(statement)" paraphrase.
0.15Perfect tense only where simple past + "recently"/"previously" would also work.
0.16Don't use "even", "any", or "own" (as in "John's own house").
0.17"all of" rather than bare "all", unless the noun is generic.
0.18Imperatives show the agent and "(imp)" — "You(John) (imp) go to the town."
0.19"begin"/"stop"/"finish"/"continue to" + verb is a feature on that verb, not its own clause.
0.20A modifier meant to apply across a coordinate noun phrase is either repeated per noun or uses "_coordinate".
0.21No isolated noun phrases with no sentence function — every noun needs a verb argument or a relation.
0.22"and" before a subordinate-looking clause is valid only as the final clause of a matching preceding type.
0.23English "of" meaning "named" — "nation named Israel", not "nation of Israel".
0.24Don't use "can" — normally becomes "is able [to ...]".
0.25"to" only starts a same-agent patient clause or a PP whose verb takes a destination — no generic "to" relation.
0.26Don't inflect hyphenated words — "John stand-up", not "John stood-up".
0.27Verbs take exactly one direct object — "gave a gift to Mary", not "gave Mary a gift".
0.28Future is "will", never "going to".
0.29No double negatives.
0.30A pairing/explication must read correctly on either its simple or complex side — target vocabulary is unknown in advance.
0.31No participles or gerunds — reword as a bracketed clause instead.
0.32"only" as adverb ("only-A", exclusivity) is now allowed — distinct from adjective "only" (3 senses: identity, exclusivity, low esteem).
0.33Avoid negative polarity on a verb combined with a causal clause ("because"/"so-A"/"so-C") — scope is ambiguous in this system.
0.34Avoid vague back-references like "did that action//thing" — be concrete unless genuinely necessary not to be.
0.36Write a given noun the same way everywhere within a sentence — no swapping in a synonym on the second mention.
0.37No passive that couldn't convert back to active without breaking another rule (e.g. the in-order-to same-agent requirement).
0.38"in prison//court" is English-specific — use "in a//the//that prison//court".
0.39English "when" that really means "after" should be written "after".
0.40No apposition except for addressees ("Lord, my(David's) God, ...").
0.41"which X" for interrogative arguments — prefer "which thing" plus the intended sense over bare "thing-A".
0.42Commas: before/after addressees, after "X said" + quote, and in coordinate lists (Oxford comma) — optional elsewhere.
0.43Avoid "let"/"allow" with God as the subordinate agent, even where English would use a natural passive.
0.44Use only the senses the system's words actually support — don't stretch a word to an unsupported sense.
0.45No "make" sense of causing an adjectival state — use "X caused [Y to be good]", not "X made Y good".
0.47"Both A and B" with two separate nouns isn't supported — "both" only goes with a dual noun.
0.48Only a verb with a patient noun can be passivized; the patient becomes the passive's agent.
0.49"obey"/"obey-submit" for laws and instructions — "follow-B" is reserved for following people.
0.50"give" for offering sacrifices — "offer" means asking if someone wants something.
0.51Imperatives with epistemic verbs (know, understand, realize) or emotion verbs are now allowed — previously discouraged.
0.52Nouns that take a noun argument ("faith in X") should use that construction rather than a relative clause.
0.53Poetry: "(begin-poetry)"/"(end-poetry)" and "(blank-line)"; "(title)" is always followed by "(paragraph)".
0.54Where units can't be used for "a short/long time", write "for a time-short-<units>" / "for a time-long-<units>".

No 0.35 or 0.46 — gaps in the source checklist's own numbering. Two entries are themselves self-noted amendments within this baseline file: 0.32 ("only" as an adverb) is marked "new," and 0.51 (imperatives with epistemic/emotion verbs) is marked "previously discouraged" — evidence the checklist had already been revised once before any of the iterations below. The checklist also references three companion documents — "How to handle complex terms", "Intro to TBTA grammar", "Summary of specialized notation" — that are now on hand in full. Cascade sources that aren't unrecognized words

phase1-encoding-review

The single most useful habit the review skill added: separate root causes from cascades before touching anything, because "because this word is not recognized, errors and warnings within the same clause may not be accurate" is the checker's own caveat. These patterns generate cascades without any word being unrecognized at all.

"where" as a relativizer (checker:32). A malformed relative gets parsed as a patient clause, producing 3–4 cascaded errors from one root cause. Fix: the "that … <adposition>" form, preposition clause-final.Joshua 4:5, Luke 5:18, Genesis 36:43 — but not guaranteed (Luke 12:18 is a counter-example): always confirm by isolating the clause.
A glued bracket ("Missing a space before [", token:syntax). One missing space can register as several separate messages.1 Kings 20:13 — two glued brackets produced 5 spurious errors; 7 total messages cleared by adding the two spaces.
Distributive "each … one <noun>". No tag fixes this one — it needs a plural rewrite as a workaround, which narrows the meaning slightly (flagged for review, not a clean fix)."Each of you(man) should carry one stone..." — Joshua 4:5
"let" without a bracket around its embedded action. Unbracketed, the embedded action reads as a second main-clause verb, so the checker reports it as a 'let' word-choice fault — "Incorrect usage of let-A" / "Unexpected patient for let-A" — plus "Cannot have multiple verbs in the same clause," all from the one missing bracket. Fix: let [X to Verb Y].Matthew 14:36 — bracketing the embedded clause alone cleared all 3 messages, 4 errors → 0, with no change to 'let' itself.
A missing bracket before an opening quote (checker:19). Beyond drawing its own error, it can make the checker misread an imperative clause inside the quote as a wrongly-nested complement clause, firing checker:8 ("Cannot mark complement clauses as imperative") on an otherwise-valid "(imp)" tag. Fix: the standard X said, ["..."] bracket-before-quote form. Observed this run, not yet folded into the skill file.Luke 23:37 (2026-09-10T15:23 run) — one added bracket cleared the checker:19 error, the checker:8 error, and 2 case-frame messages misattributed to the same missing bracket: 4 of 5 errors from one root cause.

Recurring failure patterns & notation mechanics

phase1-encoding-review + niv-to-phase1

Patterns that show up again and again across the sampled corpus rather than once per verse — worth knowing by heart rather than looking up each time.

Bare level 2/3 word (rule 0.2) is by far the most common single error — 85 of 241 errors in the 2026-09-09 200-verse sample. Repeat offenders: prophet, donkey (110 corpus rows), lamb (27), firstborn-X (21), angel, region, kingdom, holy, bull, ox, mat, righteous, neighbor, paralyzed-X, statue, disciple, teacher, redeem, ancestor, descendant, vision, apostle, scripture, chariot, sacrifice, arrest, and more. Three mechanics that trip people up: pairing order is simple/complex ("donkey/animal" fails, "animal/donkey" works); a hyphenated particle verb stays uninflected on the simple side ("take-away/arrested" works, "took-away/arrested" fails); and the rule applies inside pronoun-referent parens too — "I(prophet)" errors exactly like bare "prophet".
"all" before a non-generic noun (checker:35, rule 0.17) is a top-10 rule on its own — 12–14 errors per 200 verses in the review sample, and 15 errors across just 5 verses in niv-to-phase1's calibration sample. The generic-noun exception is real: "all his(man's) grain" passes, "all the tools" fails.
Ambiguous part of speech — tag it, don't ignore it. The "_noun"/"_verb"/"_adj"/"_adv"/"_adp"/"_conj" tags are a confirmed real fix, not noise — the tag must be space-preceded, never attached directly to the word.Exodus 13:13: "first" → "first _adv" — same pattern later confirmed on "low _adv" in 1 Kings 6:6, and on "gold _noun" in 1 Kings 10:16.
Adjective "not used" is a different failure mode from level 2/3. Shows as ontology_status: not used, level: -1 — the explication is just the replacement word (e.g. "large" → "Use 'big'"), not a pairing.
Missing comma before a quote (checker:20). Travels together with the built-in:0 capitalization rule — "Expect a , before a quote_begin clause."
Hyphenated pseudo-ontology nouns aren't entries. "man-of-God", "river-Habor", "valley-Arnon" look like single ontology terms but the checker looks up the whole hyphenated string — unhyphenate to fix.
A verb used with a role outside its theta grid. "take-away" is Agent, Patient, (Source) only — never a Destination sense — so "deported X to Assyria" must decompose into two separate events (taken from Israel; forced to go to Assyria).
Patient must sit immediately after some verbs (buy, give) — Source/Destination/Instrument come after that, not before.
An unknown proper noun directly after an adposition breaks the adposition itself. Fix with rule 0.23's "named" form and a generic head noun — "in Halah" → "in a town named Halah".
An over-nested clause inside a patient clause. Some verbs (e.g. "live") reject a different-participant patient clause — lift the descriptive relative out into its own sentence instead.
"could" outside a so-that clause needs "be able [to ...]" (P1 Checklist 2.1 in the review skill's own numbering — a different scheme from the 0.x checklist above, not yet reconciled).
Referent drift across a passage (rule 0.36).1 Kings 13:14 vs. 13:16 used different referents for the same person two verses apart.
Notation hygiene, from niv-to-phase1's mechanical pass: a space before every "[", a space after "]"/"."/")"; "_" notes are space-preceded; "|" for literal pairings; only recognized clause notations; brackets balanced.
A proper name that collides with an unrelated common noun (case-insensitively) triggers the same "ambiguous complexity" warning (checker:built-in:6) as genuine polysemy. The fix is the same sense-tag mechanic already used for common words — tag the proper name on first use — not a new rule. Observed this run, not yet folded into the skill file."Lot" (Genesis 13:10, and separately Genesis 19:15 in the same sample) collides with the unrelated common noun "lot" ("stones or sticks used like dice", level 2). Tagging the sense explicitly as "Lot-A" clears the warning immediately with no other change, confirmed by isolated /check.

Corrected understanding

phase1-encoding-review

Cases where the working understanding itself turned out to be wrong and had to be revised — not new rules, but corrections to previously-held beliefs about what's fixable.

"Acceptable residual warnings" narrowed from three items to two.
WasTreated the ambiguous-part-of-speech warning as permanently unfixable, alongside absent proper nouns and no-theta-grid-info verbs. NowTurned out fixable with a "_adv"-style tag (see the ambiguous-part-of-speech pattern above) — removed from the acceptable-residual list. Only two genuinely unfixable categories remain: real Bible proper nouns absent from the ontology (Halah, Gozan, Habor), and verbs with no theta grid information to check against ("bring-D", "be-Y").
A quirk worth remembering: the top-level status field can read "error" even with no error or warning messages actually present in the response — judge by the message list, not the status string alone.
